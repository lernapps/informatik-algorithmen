Tom's broken cards

In class 7b's group chat, after the computing lesson:

Frau Berg: Thanks for playing! Mia was Kiko and did exactly what the cards said on the board: “move forward” (one square forward), “turn left” or “turn right”.

Tom: I drew some cards of my own 😎

Mia: With “move a bit” I didn't know how far. And Kiko can't jump at all!

Here you learn four questions to check whether a set of instructions is an algorithm.

Exercise

What is the problem with Tom's card “move a bit”?

  • Everyone understands something different by it. right answer

    One square? Three squares? Up to the wall? Whoever carries out the card has to guess.

  • Kiko can't do that at all.

    Kiko can move. It's only unclear how far. That applies to “jump over the wall”.

  • Kiko then never stops.

    A bit is walked at some point. Kiko would never stop with Tom's third card.

  • The card has too many words.

    The card is short. Length doesn't matter; what matters is whether it is clear.

With “move a bit” there are several possibilities. Instructions for Kiko have to be unambiguous.

Four questions for a set of instructions

The four properties of an algorithm, each with a broken card as a counterexample
PropertyQuestion to checkBroken card
finite (finitely described)Does the list of steps come to an end?step 1, step 2, … with no end
unambiguousIs it clear at every step what to do and what comes next?“move a bit”
can be carried out (executable)Can whoever carries it out do every step, here Kiko?“jump over the wall”
terminatesIs it finished after a finite number of steps?“keep turning left again and again”
properties of an algorithm

An algorithm has four properties: it is finite (finitely described), unambiguous, can be carried out, and it terminates after a finite number of steps.

Remember

“Finite” is not the same as “terminates”. Tom's third card is short, but whoever carries it out keeps turning forever. A process that never ends doesn't solve a task that is meant to end. That is why an algorithm has to terminate.

Try it yourself

Card game “Broken cards”, without a computer, in two groups:

  1. Print the board and the command cards.
  2. Each group secretly draws a broken card and mixes it in with the proper cards.
  3. The other group plays Kiko. At the broken card they call “Stop!” and say which of the four questions fails.
  4. Fix the card together. Then swap.

Which property is the set of instructions missing?

Exercise

Mia fixes Tom's cards. Which card becomes which?

Choose the match for each entry.

Right answer: “move a bit” – “move forward”; “jump over the wall” – cards around the wall; “keep turning left again and again” – two left turns

The fixed cards only use Kiko's three commands: “move forward” instead of “a bit”, turns and steps around the wall, two left turns instead of endless ones. So they are unambiguous, can be carried out and terminate.

Exercise

Which cards can Kiko carry out on the board without guessing?

  • “move forward” right answer

    Exactly one square in the direction Kiko faces. That is clear and doable.

  • “move a few squares”

    How many is “a few”? The card is not unambiguous.

  • “turn right” right answer

    A quarter turn on the spot. That is clear and doable.

  • “fly to the goal”

    Kiko can't fly. The card cannot be carried out.

  • “turn left” right answer

    A quarter turn on the spot, just the other way. That is clear and doable too.

Kiko knows three commands. Every card that means exactly one of them, Kiko can carry out without guessing.
