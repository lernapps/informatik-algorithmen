Corridors of unknown length

In class 7b's group chat:

Selin: In my maze every corridor has a different length. With “repeat … times” it only ever works for one of them.

Ali: Kiko should just walk until the wall comes.

Frau Berg: That's exactly what “while … do” is for.

Here you learn how Kiko repeats as long as a condition is true.

Exercise

Kiko has to walk up to just before the wall in every corridor, however long the corridor is. Which program always works?

  • while way ahead clear? do move forward right answer

    Before every step Kiko checks whether the way is clear. When the wall comes, it stops, however many squares it took.

  • repeat 5 times: move forward

    That only works in a corridor with exactly 5 free squares. In a shorter one Kiko walks into the wall.

  • if way ahead clear? then move forward

    Selection only checks once. Kiko moves one square at most.

  • while wall ahead? do move forward

    The condition is the wrong way round. If the way is clear, it is false, and Kiko doesn't start at all.

When nobody knows beforehand how often, a loop with a condition helps instead of one with a number.

Repeating while the condition is true

condition-controlled loop

A condition-controlled loop runs the loop body while the condition is true. Kiko checks the condition before each pass; if it is false, the loop stops, even before the first pass.

Remember

The condition is only checked before each pass, not in the middle of the body. The block “not” turns a condition round. So the task “walk to the goal” is written like this: while not at the goal? do move forward.

infinite loop

An infinite loop never stops because its condition never becomes false. As on the page Properties: a process that never ends doesn't solve a task that is meant to end.

Try it yourself

Without a computer: lay the cards “while ___ do” and “way ahead clear?” with “move forward” inside. Someone secretly puts the wall somewhere. Before every step Kiko asks out loud: “Way ahead clear?”

With a computer, two challenges:

  • Three corridors, each a different length, with at most 3 blocks: open the challenge “How long is the corridor?”
  • Corridors with corners, a loop with selection inside: open the challenge “Around every corner”
  • Read first, then predict: open the challenge “How far does Kiko walk?”

Exercise

Three worked tasks, each with its solution:

  1. Task no. 1

    Program: while way ahead clear? do move forward. In front of Kiko there are 4 free squares, then a wall. How many times does Kiko move forward?

    Hint

    Play it through for 2 free squares. How often is the way clear?

    Solution
    1. Before each of the 4 free squares the way is clear: Kiko moves forward.
    2. After that the wall is ahead and the condition is false. Kiko moved forward 4 times.

    Right answer: 4

Exercise

How far does Kiko go with each program? Kiko faces along a straight corridor with 8 free squares, then a wall.

Choose the match for each entry.

Right answer: repeat 6 times: move forward – exactly 6 squares; while way ahead clear? do move forward – up to the wall; if way ahead clear? then move forward – exactly 1 square

The count-controlled loop runs as often as the number says. The condition-controlled loop runs while its condition is true, however often that is. Selection only checks once.
